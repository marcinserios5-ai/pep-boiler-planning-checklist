A practical checklist before replacing a boiler

A useful quotation starts with a clear brief. This checklist helps you collect information for a site visit, agree how the work will fit around your household and compare what each quotation includes. It is a preparation aid: the installer still needs to assess the property and specify the installation.

Tick a point when you have recorded it for your project.

Prepare the brief

Include the route to the work area. Photographs help with preparation; they do not replace a site assessment.

For example, note comfort concerns, available storage space and whether other trades will be working at the same time. Ask the installer which changes belong in the quotation.

Mention stairs, shared entrances, occupied rooms and times when the work area will be unavailable. Agree a practical access plan before work starts.

Compare the scope

Compare the full installation scope, rather than a headline boiler price. Keep allowances, assumptions and exclusions alongside the quotation.

Possible interfaces include electrical work, cabinetry, decoration and access panels. A written responsibility list helps prevent gaps between trades.

This may include the site assessment, equipment availability, access arrangements and the sequence of other renovation works. Record any conditions attached to the proposed dates.

Plan the working day and handover

Ask about the anticipated sequence of work, temporary interruptions and whom to contact if the schedule changes. Confirm the arrangements with the contractor rather than assuming every project follows the same timetable.

Ask what product instructions, installation records and warranty information will be supplied. Record the contact route for questions after completion.

A simple project folder makes the agreed scope easier to check. Record changes as they are agreed, including any effect on cost or programme.

Using this checklist

Take your notes to the site visit and use the same brief when comparing quotations. Keep unresolved items as questions for the contractor. Clear boundaries between included work, optional work and work by others are more useful than comparing prices without their assumptions.
